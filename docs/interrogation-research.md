# Interrogation knowledge base: sources

What the door interrogator's prompt (`lib/ai/knowledge/research.ts`) is built on. Cited from memory of the published
literature: **check each reference before relying on it in anything public.**

| Principle in the prompt | Source |
|---|---|
| Information-gathering (PEACE) beats accusatory interviewing; accusation raises false results | Meissner, Redlich, Michael, Evans, Camilletti, Bhatt & Brandon (2014), *Accusatorial and information-gathering interrogation methods and their effects on true and false confessions: a meta-analytic review*, J. Experimental Criminology |
| Accusatory (Reid-style) training raises confidence but not accuracy | Kassin & Fong (1999), *"I'm innocent!": Effects of training on judgments of truth and deception in the interrogation room*, Law and Human Behavior |
| Humans detect lies ~54% of the time | Bond & DePaulo (2006), *Accuracy of deception judgments*, Personality and Social Psychology Review |
| Nonverbal and demeanour cues are weak | DePaulo, Lindsay, Malone, Muhlenbruck, Charlton & Cooper (2003), *Cues to deception*, Psychological Bulletin |
| Truth-tellers give more verifiable detail | Nahari, Vrij & Fisher (2014), *The verifiability approach: countermeasures facilitate its ability to discriminate between truths and lies*, Applied Cognitive Psychology |
| Lying is cognitively harder; raise the load | Vrij, Granhag, Mann & Leal (2011), *Outsmarting the liars: toward a cognitive lie detection approach*, Current Directions in Psychological Science; Vrij et al. (2008), reverse-order recall, Law and Human Behavior |
| Unanticipated questions expose rehearsed stories | Vrij, Leal, Granhag, Mann, Fisher, Hillman & Sperry (2009), *Outsmarting the liars: the benefit of asking unanticipated questions*, Law and Human Behavior |
| Withhold what you know, let them commit, then check consistency | Hartwig, Granhag, Strömwall & Kronkvist (2006), *Strategic use of evidence during police interviews*, Law and Human Behavior |
| Target selection: offenders look for suitable targets with no guardian | Cohen & Felson (1979), *Social change and crime rate trends: a routine activity approach*, American Sociological Review |
| Compliance tactics: authority, scarcity, reciprocity, commitment, social proof, liking | Cialdini, *Influence: Science and Practice* |
| Doorstep-crime scripts (bogus officials, distraction burglary, rogue traders) | UK Home Office distraction-burglary research; National Trading Standards and Action Fraud doorstep-crime guidance; Age UK scam guidance |

## What this does NOT claim
- It is not "all research on criminal behaviour". It is the part that applies to a 5-20 question doorstep screen.
- The system never decides someone is a criminal. It records checkable facts, scores risk, and a human decides.
